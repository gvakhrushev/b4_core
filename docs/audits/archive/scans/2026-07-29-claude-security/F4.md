# F4: Permissionless, one-shot settle lets a third party pin another vault's minted pool weight at a price trough

**Status:** no patch produced

Declined because every available fix creates a residual worse than this LOW-severity finding. F4's harm is differential valuation timing, so closing it means either making the valuation instant uniform per interval or restricting who chooses it. Uniformity reopens the C-1 Critical by measurement, not by argument: patching opsSettle to value at the locked price reproduced the committed exploit exactly, minting 1577.97e18 of phantom weight against an honest vault's 315.59e18 — 83.3% of the basket, matching the recorded $833,333 of $1,000,000 — and even the weakest variant, clamping the live price to a plus-or-minus 10% band around the locked one, still handed the attacker 79.6%. The C-1 exemption for pinning price and composition to the same instant does not rescue this, because the report window is exactly where Calendar.targetAt ramps and the permissionless crank mandates a composition change. On the caller-restriction side: the owner window is the rejected attempt; owner-only would make Keeper.settleVault revert into its catch forever; re-settle best-of gives every active owner a repeatable per-interval mint against passive co-claimants; re-settle last-write-wins lets a third party force repeated irreversible in-kind operator payments at chosen peaks; and a pool consensus-price band lets an attacker settle first at a wick and lock every other vault out of the interval entirely, which is critical-grade grief. No TWAP source exists — CoreReader's spot, mark and oracle reads are all instantaneous precompile reads. The honest remaining remedy is a documentation amendment the product owner must decide on: spec/SECURITY_MODEL.md:101-104 and docs/05-security.md:80 justify the settle-timing residual partly on the keeper settling a pool's vaults in a single pass at one block and one price, and F4 is exactly the case where a hostile caller pre-empts that pass, so uniformity is an operational expectation rather than a code guarantee and the clause should say so. Widening a recorded accepted residual is a risk-acceptance decision, so no edit was made.

## The claim that could not be made with confidence

- **the change introduces no new security vulnerability** -- NOT_CONFIDENT: the owner-window attempt refused the keeper's single-pass settle for the first two days of every window, handing each owner a guaranteed unpreemptable slice to pick their own valuation peak against passive co-claimants
- **beyond closing the finding, the change does not alter the code's behaviour or the inputs it accepts** -- NOT_CONFIDENT: with the gate enabled, Keeper.crank reported no weight at all during the front of the report window (test_full_cycle_all_products failed 0 <= 0)

## The report's original recommendation

Remove the caller's discretion over the valuation instant: either value the interval at the interval's locked checkpoint price (restoring `lockPrices` as the canonical, owner-preemptable basis) or reserve `settle` for the vault owner for the first portion of the report window and open it to keepers only near the deadline, so a third party can supply liveness but cannot choose the price. Note that both halves of this recommendation were implemented and measured: the first reopens C-1 and the second creates a repeatable owner-side advantage, so neither is a drop-in fix.
