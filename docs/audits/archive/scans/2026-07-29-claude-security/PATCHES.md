## No patch produced

- **F1** -- Exit-weight forfeiture is gated on exact `keep == 0`, so `initiateExit(WAD - 1)` withdraws ~100% of a vault's capital while keeping its full reported pool weight: Two attempts, both rejected. The first made forfeiture strictly proportional and repriced every ordinary partial exit — a 50% exit lost half its weight — which reverses an economic decision the repository made deliberately (docs/audits/REVIEW-2026-07-25-agent-changes.md:418-431, INVARIANTS.md:82, and the AuditHalfB docstring 'a PARTIAL exit is not a departure'). The second attempt narrowed it to a materiality floor measured on the post-exit base, but the verifier executed the exploit against the patched sources and it still worked: rewardBaseWad = (R + C*x)*keep is re-inflated by the departing capital's own profit, so with unsettled profit a vault paid out 99.2% of its holdings and kept 100% of its reported weight, splitting the basket 50/50 with a fully-invested stayer. The finding is real and remains open; closing it needs a measure the pool can verify itself rather than a caller-supplied post-exit base whose inflation term the owner controls.
- **F2** -- Peak-anchor daily sample slot can be squatted, suppressing peakC and over-levering every structural short: Declined after analysis showed F2 cannot be closed inside `sampleAnchor` without reversing a documented policy decision. F2 (suppression) and the M-3 wick attack (inflation) are the same permissionless daily race seen from its two sides. Three existing pinned assertions make the rule complete and contradictory: AnchorRatchet.t.sol:218 and AuditH4M3_Anchors require that a single observation at least a day after the last counted sample MUST bind the value; test_M3_intraday_wick_cannot_move_the_confirmed_peak requires that an observation less than a day after it MUST NOT bind. Closing F2 requires that a later same-day observation at the true high MUST bind — the same on-chain event sequence as the second rule, demanding the opposite outcome. The only differing fields are msg.sender (free to create) and the elapsed time, so no witness or dwell threshold separates the cases; it only prices the wick. This is why the verifier's reproduction succeeded — its probe is the F2 attack with the roles swapped, so any patch admitting the honest high must admit the probe. Detect-and-withhold is also unavailable: with the peak withheld, shortStructLev rises to 1.726x versus 1.279x for the suppressed value, so withholding is more levered than the harm it would replace. The three ways forward are all owner policy calls: accept F2 as a residual; adopt the low side's monotone ratchet, closing F2 and accepting M-3; or change the trust model with a registered sampler set or a non-instantaneous daily close, both of which have dated precedent against them (HAZARDS G2 and section 4.15; the second-oracle dependency declined at spec/HAZARDS.md:180 C4 on 2026-07-18).
- **F4** -- Permissionless, one-shot settle lets a third party pin another vault's minted pool weight at a price trough: Declined because every available fix creates a residual worse than this LOW-severity finding. F4's harm is differential valuation timing, so closing it means either making the valuation instant uniform per interval or restricting who chooses it. Uniformity reopens the C-1 Critical by measurement, not by argument: patching opsSettle to value at the locked price reproduced the committed exploit exactly, minting 1577.97e18 of phantom weight against an honest vault's 315.59e18 — 83.3% of the basket, matching the recorded $833,333 of $1,000,000 — and even the weakest variant, clamping the live price to a plus-or-minus 10% band around the locked one, still handed the attacker 79.6%. The C-1 exemption for pinning price and composition to the same instant does not rescue this, because the report window is exactly where Calendar.targetAt ramps and the permissionless crank mandates a composition change. On the caller-restriction side: the owner window is the rejected attempt; owner-only would make Keeper.settleVault revert into its catch forever; re-settle best-of gives every active owner a repeatable per-interval mint against passive co-claimants; re-settle last-write-wins lets a third party force repeated irreversible in-kind operator payments at chosen peaks; and a pool consensus-price band lets an attacker settle first at a wick and lock every other vault out of the interval entirely, which is critical-grade grief. No TWAP source exists — CoreReader's spot, mark and oracle reads are all instantaneous precompile reads. The honest remaining remedy is a documentation amendment the product owner must decide on: spec/SECURITY_MODEL.md:101-104 and docs/05-security.md:80 justify the settle-timing residual partly on the keeper settling a pool's vaults in a single pass at one block and one price, and F4 is exactly the case where a hostile caller pre-empts that pass, so uniformity is an operational expectation rather than a code guarantee and the clause should say so. Widening a recorded accepted residual is a risk-acceptance decision, so no edit was made.
